The problem, and the constraint it forces

On screenLanding view of the app, static. Do not click anything yet — let them read the name.

India's DPDP Act 2023 gives every data principal a statutory right to erasure under Section 12. Exercising it is the hard part: you have to identify which entities hold you, know which of them the right actually reaches, serve a valid notice, and then prove deletion.

Our hardest design constraint falls straight out of that problem. A name identifies nobody. A privacy tool that guesses from names helps you demand the deletion of a stranger's records.

What it actually is

On screenScroll once through the Privacy Agent tab — identity panel left, radar and trace right. Keep it slow.

ApniPehChaan is an autonomous multi-agent system, not a scanner. A scanner runs a fixed pipeline and hands you a table, leaving you the entire legal burden. Our agent runs a closed loop — discover, judge, act, verify — across twenty-four tools and three specialist sub-agents: Forensics, Legal Counsel, and Remediation.

Every intelligence source is free and keyless, so the product's central question works with no card on file.

Live run — discovery and the honesty rules

On screenClick Deploy Privacy Agent. Let the radar sweep and the trace stream. When results land, scroll to Exposure Ledger, then stop on the "Is this you?" panel — that is your strongest visual proof, so hold on it while you say the last line.

I'll deploy the agent against my own identity. On the left is the sovereign identity vault; on the right, the live threat radar and the swarm's reasoning trace, streaming as it happens — there is no sleep padding in that feed.

Forensics queries XposedOrNot, Hudson Rock for info-stealer infections, LeakCheck and ProxyNova combination lists, and Pwned Passwords using k-anonymity — only the first five characters of the SHA-1 hash ever leave this machine.

Two things to watch. When a source rate-limits us, the agent reports the sweep as incomplete — it never returns "clear" for a check that could not run.

And this panel is the constraint made visible. Anything matched on weaker evidence sits here as an unconfirmed candidate — held out of the ledger, the risk score and the removal plan until I confirm it is me. Attribution needs an exact unique identifier: email, Indian mobile, PAN, Aadhaar or UPI.

Legal Studio — judgement and the approval gate

On screenOpen Legal Studio. Type a company name into the chat header so the two-way sync updates the recipient and statute live. Then show the drafted notice sitting unsent.

Now the judgement layer. The Legal Counsel agent resolves an arbitrary company to its registered legal entity and Grievance Officer dynamically — there is no hardcoded list behind this.

It also distinguishes what the law actually reaches. An operating fiduciary is servable under Section 12. A court record is not — that needs an application to the issuing court. MCA filings are excluded under Section 3(c)(ii) as data published under a legal duty.

It drafts the notice, and stops. Dispatch is withheld from the discovery toolset entirely, so the agent cannot send. Only I can.

Compliance, and the engineering close

On screenApprove one notice. Show the Compliance section: statutory clock, milestones, SHA-256 receipt. End on the receipt or the passing test run.

On approval the notice goes out and the statutory clock starts — thirty days under DPDP, forty-five under CCPA. Every action mints a SHA-256 receipt in a tamper-evident hash chain. If the fiduciary misses the deadline, the agent escalates to the Data Protection Board of India.

Underneath: FastAPI, Supabase Postgres, deployed on Vercel, three hundred and forty-six automated tests. The discipline running through all of it is refusal — the system will not claim a removal it hasn't verified, will not serve a notice the law doesn't support, and will not attribute a record on a name. Thank you.

Do not claim "zero false positives" as an absolute. Say what is actually true and stronger: attribution requires an exact unique identifier, and a legal name is never used to attribute or to search. It is a design rule you can show in the code, not a statistic you would have to defend.

Do not demo the full removal loop end to end. Real controllers take weeks and require identity verification. Show discovery, judgement, and the notice held at the approval gate — that is the honest scope and it is enough.

Delivery notes

  • Pace is 150 words per minute. If you naturally run fast, the 9-second buffer absorbs it; if you run slow, cut the second sentence of beat two.
  • The two highlighted lines are the ones judges remember — "a name identifies nobody" and "the agent cannot send". Land them with a half-second pause after.
  • Narrate over the scan rather than waiting for it. Dead air while a radar sweeps is the most common way these run long.
  • If a live source rate-limits mid-demo, do not apologise — point at it. The agent reporting "could not check" instead of "clear" is the thesis of the build, demonstrating itself.
  • Record system audio so the judges hear nothing but you. Mute notifications.
